The STAR Method for Interview Answers

Any question that starts with “tell me about a time” is a behavioral question. The STAR method keeps your answer clear and complete.

Why interviewers ask it

Behavioral questions predict how you will act in the job, so interviewers want real examples with real results.

A simple way to structure your answer

  1. Situation: Set the scene in one or two sentences.
  2. Task: What you were responsible for.
  3. Action: What you did. This should be most of your answer. Say “I”, not “we”.
  4. Result: What happened, with a number if possible.

Example answer

Situation: Our clinic was getting complaints about long phone hold times. Task: I was asked to help reduce them. Action: I tracked when calls peaked, moved our reminder calls to quieter hours and set up a voicemail callback list. Result: Average hold time dropped from six minutes to under two within a month.

Make it yours: swap in your own job, numbers and goals.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping the result
  • Talking about what the team did instead of you
  • Telling a story that goes on for five minutes

Practice your interview out loud

Reading answers isn't the same as saying them. Pick your interview stage, answer Jane's questions out loud, and get feedback on your answer, pace and filler words. Then try again.
