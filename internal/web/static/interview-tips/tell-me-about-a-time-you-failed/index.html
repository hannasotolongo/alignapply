How to Answer “Tell Me About a Time You Failed”

Everyone has failed at something. What matters is that you own it and learned from it.

Why interviewers ask it

They want to see honesty, accountability and growth.

A simple way to structure your answer

  1. Situation: A real, work-related failure. Keep it brief.
  2. Ownership: What you did wrong, in one sentence.
  3. Recovery and lesson: How you fixed it and what you do differently now.

Example answer

I once sent a schedule to the whole team with the wrong dates. I caught it within the hour, sent a correction and called the two people it affected most. Since then I always double-check anything going to more than one person before I send it.

Make it yours: swap in your own job, numbers and goals.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing a fake failure
  • Blaming others
  • Spending most of the answer on the failure instead of the fix

Practice your interview out loud

Reading answers isn't the same as saying them. Pick your interview stage, answer Jane's questions out loud, and get feedback on your answer, pace and filler words. Then try again.
