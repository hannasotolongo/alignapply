Software Engineer (behavioral) Interview Questions and Answers

Beyond coding rounds, software engineer interviews include behavioral questions about ownership, collaboration and handling ambiguity. Here are the 10 questions you're most likely to hear, and how to answer each one.

Tip: for any “tell me about a time” question, use the STAR method.

1. Tell me about yourself.

What they want to know: A quick, relevant summary that shows why you fit this role.

How to answer: Use present, past, future: what you do now, one or two wins that match this job, and why this role is your next step. Keep it under two minutes.

2. Why do you want to work here?

What they want to know: That you picked them on purpose, not at random.

How to answer: Name one specific thing about the company (its patients, customers, product or reputation) and connect it to what you enjoy doing.

3. Tell me about the most challenging project you've worked on.

What they want to know: Depth and ownership.

How to answer: Explain the technical challenge, your specific role, the trade-offs you made, and the measurable result.

4. Describe a time you disagreed with a teammate on a technical decision.

What they want to know: Collaboration.

How to answer: Show how you used data or a quick prototype, listened, and either committed or changed your mind.

5. Tell me about a bug you caused in production.

What they want to know: Accountability.

How to answer: Own it, explain how you fixed it fast, and the test or process you added so it would not happen again.

6. How do you handle unclear requirements?

What they want to know: Dealing with ambiguity.

How to answer: Ask clarifying questions, write down assumptions, and ship a small version to get feedback.

7. How do you approach code reviews?

What they want to know: Team quality.

How to answer: Mention being kind and specific, explaining the why, and being open to feedback on your own code.

8. Tell me about a time you had to learn a new technology quickly.

What they want to know: Learning speed.

How to answer: Share what you learned, how, and what you built with it.

9. How do you balance speed and quality?

What they want to know: Judgment.

How to answer: Explain choosing based on risk: move fast on reversible changes, be careful on critical paths.

10. Why do you want to join our team?

What they want to know: Motivation.

How to answer: Connect their product or technical challenges to what you want to work on.

How to answer the classics

  • Tell me about yourself
  • Why do you want to work here
  • What is your greatest weakness
  • What is your greatest strength
  • Why should we hire you
