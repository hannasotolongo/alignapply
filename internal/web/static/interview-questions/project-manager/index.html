Project Manager Interview Questions and Answers

Project manager interviews test how you plan, communicate, manage risk and keep people aligned when things go off track. Here are the 10 questions you're most likely to hear, and how to answer each one.

Tip: for any “tell me about a time” question, use the STAR method.

1. Tell me about yourself.

What they want to know: A quick, relevant summary that shows why you fit this role.

How to answer: Use present, past, future: what you do now, one or two wins that match this job, and why this role is your next step. Keep it under two minutes.

2. Why do you want to work here?

What they want to know: That you picked them on purpose, not at random.

How to answer: Name one specific thing about the company (its patients, customers, product or reputation) and connect it to what you enjoy doing.

3. Walk me through a project you managed from start to finish.

What they want to know: Your process and results.

How to answer: Cover the goal, scope, team, timeline, a challenge you hit, and the measurable result.

4. Tell me about a project that went off track. What did you do?

What they want to know: Recovery and honesty.

How to answer: Explain how you spotted the problem early, re-planned, told stakeholders, and what you learned.

5. How do you handle scope creep?

What they want to know: Protecting the plan.

How to answer: Describe evaluating the impact, discussing trade-offs with stakeholders, and using a change process.

6. How do you keep stakeholders informed?

What they want to know: Communication habits.

How to answer: Mention regular status updates, a clear dashboard, and adjusting detail for executives versus the team.

7. How do you handle a team member who keeps missing deadlines?

What they want to know: People management.

How to answer: Talk privately, find the cause, agree on support or changes, and follow up.

8. What project tools and methods do you use?

What they want to know: Fit with their way of working.

How to answer: Name tools (Jira, Asana, MS Project, Smartsheet) and methods (Agile, Scrum, Waterfall), and when you use each.

9. How do you manage risks?

What they want to know: Thinking ahead.

How to answer: Explain keeping a risk list, rating likelihood and impact, and having plans for the biggest ones.

10. How do you prioritize competing projects?

What they want to know: Judgment.

How to answer: Describe aligning with business goals and agreeing priorities with leadership.

How to answer the classics

  • Tell me about yourself
  • Why do you want to work here
  • What is your greatest weakness
  • What is your greatest strength
  • Why should we hire you
