Medical Assistant Interview Questions and Answers

Medical assistant interviews test both clinical basics and how you treat patients, especially when the clinic is busy or a patient is upset. Here are the 10 questions you're most likely to hear, and how to answer each one.

Tip: for any “tell me about a time” question, use the STAR method.

1. Tell me about yourself.

What they want to know: A quick, relevant summary that shows why you fit this role.

How to answer: Use present, past, future: what you do now, one or two wins that match this job, and why this role is your next step. Keep it under two minutes.

2. Why do you want to work here?

What they want to know: That you picked them on purpose, not at random.

How to answer: Name one specific thing about the company (its patients, customers, product or reputation) and connect it to what you enjoy doing.

3. Why did you choose to become a medical assistant?

What they want to know: Real motivation for patient care.

How to answer: Share a short personal reason and connect it to what you like about the day-to-day work: helping patients feel comfortable and keeping the clinic moving.

4. What clinical skills are you comfortable performing?

What they want to know: What you can do on day one.

How to answer: Be specific and honest: vitals, injections, phlebotomy, EKGs, rooming patients, and any certification (CMA, RMA, CCMA).

5. How do you handle an anxious or upset patient?

What they want to know: Empathy and calm under pressure.

How to answer: Explain how you stay calm, listen, explain each step, and give a real example of turning a nervous patient around.

6. Which EHR systems have you used?

What they want to know: How fast you can get up to speed.

How to answer: Name systems you know (Epic, Cerner, athenahealth, eClinicalWorks) and say you learn new ones quickly, with an example.

7. How do you make sure you follow HIPAA?

What they want to know: That you protect patient privacy every day.

How to answer: Mention verifying identity, never discussing patients in public areas, locking screens, and only accessing records you need.

8. Tell me about a time you worked in a fast-paced clinic.

What they want to know: Whether you stay accurate when rushed.

How to answer: Describe a full schedule, how you kept rooms turning over, and how you double-checked medications or charts while moving fast.

9. How would you handle a patient who refuses a procedure?

What they want to know: Respect for patient rights and good communication.

How to answer: Say you would stay respectful, make sure they understand, answer what you can, and let the provider know right away.

10. How do you work with physicians and nurses?

What they want to know: Teamwork and anticipating needs.

How to answer: Give an example of preparing a room or supplies before the provider asked, and how you communicate updates during the day.

How to answer the classics

  • Tell me about yourself
  • Why do you want to work here
  • What is your greatest weakness
  • What is your greatest strength
  • Why should we hire you
