Data Analyst Interview Questions and Answers

Data analyst interviews mix technical questions with how you turn data into clear recommendations people act on. Here are the 10 questions you're most likely to hear, and how to answer each one.

Tip: for any “tell me about a time” question, use the STAR method.

1. Tell me about yourself.

What they want to know: A quick, relevant summary that shows why you fit this role.

How to answer: Use present, past, future: what you do now, one or two wins that match this job, and why this role is your next step. Keep it under two minutes.

2. Why do you want to work here?

What they want to know: That you picked them on purpose, not at random.

How to answer: Name one specific thing about the company (its patients, customers, product or reputation) and connect it to what you enjoy doing.

3. Walk me through an analysis you did that changed a decision.

What they want to know: Business impact.

How to answer: Explain the question, the data, your method, what you found, and what the team did differently.

4. How do you clean messy data?

What they want to know: Your process.

How to answer: Mention checking for duplicates, missing values and outliers, documenting changes, and validating results.

5. Which tools do you use most?

What they want to know: Technical fit.

How to answer: Be specific: SQL (joins, window functions), Excel, Python or R, and Tableau or Power BI.

6. How do you explain findings to non-technical people?

What they want to know: Communication.

How to answer: Start with the answer, use one simple chart, and focus on what it means for them.

7. Tell me about a time your analysis was wrong.

What they want to know: Honesty and rigor.

How to answer: Explain how you found the error, corrected it, told stakeholders, and the check you added.

8. How do you decide which metrics matter?

What they want to know: Business thinking.

How to answer: Tie metrics to the goal of the team, and explain leading versus lagging indicators.

9. How would you investigate a sudden drop in sales?

What they want to know: Problem-solving.

How to answer: Break it down by time, region, product and channel, check for data issues first, then find the driver.

10. How do you prioritize requests from different teams?

What they want to know: Judgment.

How to answer: Weigh business impact and urgency, and agree on priorities with your manager.

How to answer the classics

  • Tell me about yourself
  • Why do you want to work here
  • What is your greatest weakness
  • What is your greatest strength
  • Why should we hire you
