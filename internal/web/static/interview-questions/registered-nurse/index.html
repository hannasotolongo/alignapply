Registered Nurse Interview Questions and Answers

Nursing interviews focus on patient safety, clinical judgment, teamwork and how you handle stress. Expect many 'tell me about a time' questions. Here are the 10 questions you're most likely to hear, and how to answer each one.

Tip: for any “tell me about a time” question, use the STAR method.

1. Tell me about yourself.

What they want to know: A quick, relevant summary that shows why you fit this role.

How to answer: Use present, past, future: what you do now, one or two wins that match this job, and why this role is your next step. Keep it under two minutes.

2. Why do you want to work here?

What they want to know: That you picked them on purpose, not at random.

How to answer: Name one specific thing about the company (its patients, customers, product or reputation) and connect it to what you enjoy doing.

3. Tell me about a time you advocated for a patient.

What they want to know: That you speak up for patient safety.

How to answer: Use STAR: what you noticed, how you raised it with the provider or team, and how it changed the patient's care.

4. How do you prioritize care when you have several patients?

What they want to know: Clinical judgment.

How to answer: Explain how you assess acuity (for example ABCs, unstable before stable) and give an example from a busy shift.

5. Describe a time you made a mistake or a near miss.

What they want to know: Honesty and a safety culture.

How to answer: Own it briefly, explain how you reported and fixed it, and the change you made afterwards.

6. How do you handle a difficult patient or family member?

What they want to know: Communication and empathy.

How to answer: Describe listening, setting clear expectations, involving the charge nurse or social work if needed, and a real example.

7. How do you deal with stress on a hard shift?

What they want to know: Resilience and self-care.

How to answer: Share what you do during and after a shift to stay steady, and how you lean on your team.

8. Tell me about a conflict with a coworker and how you resolved it.

What they want to know: Teamwork.

How to answer: Choose a professional disagreement, explain how you talked it through privately, and the outcome for patients.

9. How do you stay current with best practices?

What they want to know: Commitment to learning.

How to answer: Mention continuing education, certifications (BLS, ACLS, specialty certifications), journals or unit training.

10. Why this unit or specialty?

What they want to know: Fit and long-term interest.

How to answer: Connect your experience and what you enjoy to the unit's patients and pace.

  • Why do you want to work here
  • What is your greatest weakness
  • What is your greatest strength
  • Why should we hire you
