Office Manager Interview Questions and Answers

Office manager interviews focus on organization, juggling priorities, handling people calmly and keeping the office running without being asked twice. Here are the 10 questions you're most likely to hear, and how to answer each one.

Tip: for any “tell me about a time” question, use the STAR method.

1. Tell me about yourself.

What they want to know: A quick, relevant summary that shows why you fit this role.

How to answer: Use present, past, future: what you do now, one or two wins that match this job, and why this role is your next step. Keep it under two minutes.

2. Why do you want to work here?

What they want to know: That you picked them on purpose, not at random.

How to answer: Name one specific thing about the company (its patients, customers, product or reputation) and connect it to what you enjoy doing.

3. How do you prioritize when everything feels urgent?

What they want to know: Whether you have a system, or just react.

How to answer: Describe how you sort tasks by deadline and impact, then give a real example of a busy day and what you handled first and why.

4. Tell me about a time you improved a process in the office.

What they want to know: That you notice problems and fix them without being told.

How to answer: Use STAR: the messy situation, what you changed (a new filing system, a shared calendar, a supply checklist), and the result in time or money saved.

5. How would you handle a conflict between two coworkers?

What they want to know: Calm, fair judgment and knowing when to escalate.

How to answer: Explain that you would listen to each person privately, find the actual issue, agree on a next step, and bring in a manager if it involves policy or keeps happening.

6. What software and tools are you comfortable with?

What they want to know: Whether you can start working right away.

How to answer: List the tools you really use (Microsoft Office or Google Workspace, Excel, scheduling or billing systems, QuickBooks) and one thing you did with each.

7. How do you handle vendors and office budgets?

What they want to know: Trustworthiness with money and suppliers.

How to answer: Share how you track spending, compare quotes, and an example of money you saved or a vendor problem you solved.

8. Describe a time you made a mistake. What happened?

What they want to know: Honesty and how you recover.

How to answer: Pick a real but small mistake, own it in one sentence, then spend most of the answer on how you fixed it and what you do differently now.

9. How do you keep confidential information secure?

What they want to know: Discretion with HR, payroll and client details.

How to answer: Mention locking files and screens, sharing on a need-to-know basis, and following company policy, with an example if you have one.

10. Where do you see yourself in a few years?

What they want to know: Whether you will stay and grow.

How to answer: Show ambition that fits this job: taking on more responsibility, leading a team or owning operations, here.

How to answer the classics

  • Tell me about yourself
  • Why do you want to work here
  • What is your greatest weakness
  • What is your greatest strength
  • Why should we hire you
